# Does group membership suppress individual growth

This study sets out to test a public claim by KSI that YouTube group membership suppresses individual creator growth and turns the space into a monopoly. The claim is specific and stated as a general law, and it decomposes into four sub-claims that can resolve differently: that pair collaborations are reciprocal, that group collaborations are not, that members therefore receive exposure without traffic because the group channel captures the views, and that groups suppress individual growth and concentrate it (docs/BACKGROUND.md). Every input the claim rests on is public, so the argument is measurable. The analysis covers five estimation groups (Sidemen, OfflineTV, Beta Squad, 2HYPE, AMP) at the level of the individual long-form video, and it reports each sub-claim with the precision achieved rather than settling the claim in either direction.

## Reciprocity is asymmetric

The first two sub-claims concern who appears on whose channel. A reciprocity ledger counts same-group appearances by direction: member-to-group counts videos a group-role channel uploads with a member present, and group-to-member counts videos a member-role channel uploads with the group channel present (outputs/figures/reciprocity_ledger.svg; outputs/tables/reciprocity_ledger.csv). Across the five groups the totals are 20235 member-to-group appearances against 658 in the other direction. The asymmetry holds for four of the five groups, with per-group member-to-group counts of 17822 for Sidemen, 829 for OfflineTV, 820 for Beta Squad, and 490 for AMP against 7, 2, 75, and 5 returns respectively (docs/FINDINGS.md). 2HYPE reverses the pattern, at 274 member-to-group against 569 group-to-member, its group channel inactive in recent years. Detection is alias-based and Sidemen-dense, so the near-absence of group-to-member content is a floor observation rather than a proven absence, but at this coverage the ledger is consistent with the reciprocity premise of the claim.

## The group channel captures a large view share

The third sub-claim is that the group channel captures the views the members generate for it. Attention share measures the group-role channels' share of summed ecosystem views by video publish year (outputs/figures/attention_share.svg; outputs/tables/attention_share.csv). The share is near zero in the years before each group's formation, when only member channels exist, and by 2026 it reaches 0.456 for Sidemen across 3 group and 6 member channels, 0.693 for Beta Squad, 0.632 for AMP, and 0.253 for OfflineTV, with 2HYPE at 0.000 as its group channel was inactive that year (docs/FINDINGS.md). At the ecosystem level a group channel commanding a plurality to majority of views is consistent with exposure landing on the group channel. View count is cumulative at a single snapshot, so the level carries the accrual caveat set out in METHODS.md, though the within-year cross-channel share largely cancels the common accrual factor. The descriptive half of the claim, that members feed a channel that captures the resulting attention, therefore holds in the data.

## Individual performance moves in the wrong direction for the claim

The substantive sub-claim is that group membership suppresses individual growth. Q4 estimates the association between membership and log(views) per long-form video on a member's own channel, using a Sun and Abraham event study with channel and calendar-month fixed effects and a wild cluster bootstrap over the five ecosystems (METHODS.md). Reading it causally requires parallel trends, and the pre-trend test rejects that assumption. The sup-t statistic across the 23 pre-periods over relative months [-24, -2] is 7.672 with p 0.001 at 5 clusters, and the event-study path is not flat before treatment, running from -1.030 at month -23 up to 0.308 at month -8 (outputs/figures/did_event_study.svg; outputs/tables/did_pretrend_test.csv, outputs/tables/did_event_study.csv). A failed pre-trend is the finding, and per PROJECT_PLAN Section 12 the specification is not changed in response.

Read as an association under that violated assumption, the overall post-treatment estimate is 0.339 log points, 95 percent interval [0.194, 0.485], standard error 0.088, over 5 ecosystem clusters, 30 treated channels, and 12342 videos (docs/FINDINGS.md; outputs/tables/did_overall_att.csv). The interval excludes zero, so membership is associated with higher log(views) per video on average, the opposite sign from the one the claim predicts. Against a pre-registered prior of near zero and imprecise, the observed estimate is positive with an interval excluding zero, though the violated pre-trend caps what the sign can be read to mean.

## The size split does not recover the general law

Q5 asks whether the sign depends on pre-join size, which is where the claim could hold as a general law: if membership subsidises small members with exposure and taxes large ones whose views the group channel cannibalises, KSI's claim would be correct for a large member such as himself and incorrect in general. Arms split at the median of the pre-treatment 12-month mean log(views), a split value of 13.010 (outputs/figures/did_q5_split.svg; outputs/tables/did_q5_heterogeneity.csv). The large arm estimate is 0.145, 95 percent interval [0.011, 0.280], standard error 0.079, 14 treated channels and 7056 videos, and the small arm is 0.579, 95 percent interval [0.416, 0.748], standard error 0.099, 13 treated channels and 5246 videos (docs/FINDINGS.md). Both arms are positive and the small arm is larger, which is consistent with exposure benefiting smaller members more. The general law requires large members to lose views, and at this precision the large arm is positive, so the data does not support growth suppression for large members. Under the violated pre-trend both arms are associations.

## The named cases add no support

Q6 examines two named cases with synthetic control, on the reasoning that if membership suppressed an individual's views, an exit or a collapse would release them. For the KSI exit of 2026-05-31 the observable post window is 9.7 weeks and 3 monthly observations under a relaxed age filter, the pre-event RMSPE is 0.932 log points, and the post gap of 0.318 ranks 41st of 41 units, the least anomalous in the pool, so the case is described and not read as an effect (docs/FINDINGS.md; outputs/tables/synth_fit.csv, outputs/tables/synth_placebo.csv). For the Team 10 collapse, with Jake Paul as the treated unit and a close pre-fit RMSPE of 0.216, the post gap is -0.195 log points ranking 15th of 41 (p 0.366), a negative gap that cannot be distinguished from the donor placebo distribution. Neither case recovers the release of views the general law implies.

## What the analysis supports

Read across the six questions, the evidence is associational and does not establish the claim as proven or refuted. The descriptive premises hold: reciprocity is asymmetric for four of five groups, and the group channel commands a plurality to majority share of 2026 views. Its central prediction does not: the Q4 and Q5 associations are positive rather than negative, the small-member arm exceeds the large-member arm, the pre-trend test rejects parallel trends, and neither named case recovers a suppression effect. The results are consistent with the descriptive half of the claim and inconsistent with its central prediction, though the violated identifying assumption keeps the reading an association rather than a treatment effect, and the precision is bounded by the five ecosystem clusters rather than the video count. Looking ahead, the two directions that would sharpen the reading are to reconstruct daily view velocity from the committed snapshot series, which would narrow the accrual caveat, and to add treatment clusters or adopt a design stable at few clusters, which would tighten every interval reported here.
